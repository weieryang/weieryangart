FAQ

How should the stone and sample be agreed for an outdoor sculpture?

Identify the proposed stone and finish, with a sample reference for colour, texture and acceptable natural variation. Provide the site's weather, water exposure, public use and care access so the project team can review material suitability. Agree any material documentation or tests required for this commission before approval; a photograph or stone name alone is not a performance guarantee.

What should be approved before marble carving or other stone fabrication?

Start from references you are authorised to share, and identify the drawing or model revision, dimensions and unresolved details. Record which views, proportions, surface details, base interfaces and samples require approval. For OEM work, identify the client artwork, any design-development deliverables and who may approve changes before fabrication.

Who reviews support, lifting and drainage for a stone sculpture or base?

Provide the site plan, proposed sculpture and base geometry, receiving route, lifting access and water or drainage context. Agree what sculpture-side interface information is included and who reviews local support, foundations, anchors, lifting and installation. These decisions need the actual project information and appointed site specialists; a reference image is not an installation method.

What should a custom stone sculpture or OEM quotation include?

Compare the same design revision, dimensions, proposed stone and finish, sample approvals and fabrication scope. Then list packing, freight, delivery point, handling, local foundations, lifting, assembly and guidance as included, excluded or still to confirm. Identify provisional design items and the approval process for changes; a reference photograph is not a fixed-price specification.

How should care and handover requirements be agreed?

Discuss care access and the proposed cleaning approach for the selected stone, finish and actual site with the project team. Agree the required sample and drawing approvals, material records, condition photographs, care notes and any packing or assembly instructions. Name the party responsible after delivery. Do not treat a generic outdoor lifespan or another project's photograph as a guarantee for this commission.
