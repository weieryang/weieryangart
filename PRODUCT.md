# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Overseas architects, landscape designers, hospitality developers, public-art consultants, and procurement teams evaluating a custom sculpture supplier for large private, commercial, and civic projects. They arrive with site context, material preferences, delivery constraints, and a need to judge whether the supplier can handle design, engineering, fabrication, shipping, and installation guidance.

## Product Purpose

The site positions the company as a premium custom sculpture and public-art fabrication partner. Success means a qualified buyer understands the studio's taste level, engineering competence, material range, and inquiry path within the first visit, then submits a project brief or starts a quote conversation.

## Brand Personality

Precise, architectural, and quietly rare. The brand should feel like a private atelier that can also pass a contractor's technical review.

## Anti-references

Avoid traditional low-trust factory websites, crowded product marketplaces, generic AI luxury templates, beige editorial sameness, purple SaaS gradients, cheap metric blocks, identical card grids, and any complex generated sculpture image that looks physically impossible or shows object interpenetration.

## Design Principles

- Lead with evidence, not decoration: materials, drawings, process, and project constraints should carry trust.
- Make the inquiry path feel like a curated project intake, not a cheap quote form.
- Use fewer, stronger visual moments; one credible material image beats five questionable renderings.
- Keep luxury quiet: typography, spacing, contrast, and restraint should do more work than effects.
- Treat engineering as a premium signal, not back-office detail.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, keyboard-accessible controls, visible focus states, reduced-motion support, 44px touch targets on mobile, readable body text at 16px or larger, and robust handling for long country, material, and project-type values.
