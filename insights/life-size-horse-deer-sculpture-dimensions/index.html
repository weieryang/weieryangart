How Do You Size a Life-Size Horse or Deer Sculpture?

Published November 25, 2026By WEIERYANG
Specify the animal and the space it occupies

For a life-size animal sculpture, give the fabricator two sets of dimensions. One describes the animal you want to recognise. The other gives the full height, width and depth of the finished pose, including a raised hoof, wings or antlers. Show the base separately. The same animal can occupy very different spaces when its pose changes.

What does “life-size” mean for a horse?

A standing horse’s height is measured at the withers, the ridge where the neck meets the back. Iowa State University’s equine guidance identifies that point on a standing horse. For a sculpture brief, use a known horse or a measured reference to establish the body scale. A head raised in profile or a wing lifted above it changes the top of the artwork. See the equine measurement illustration.

The workshop photograph shows a rearing winged horse. A designer who writes only “two metres high” could mean the body size in a standing reference, the top of a wing, or the distance from the finished floor. Draw an arrow with a start and end point beside the number. Give wingspan its own line on the brief.

How should you describe a deer pair?

In the outdoor photograph, the antlers extend well above the neighbouring animal. Identify each figure on the drawing. Give each a body dimension, then add the tip height and the distance between the two animals from a named point on each. The gap seen from the visitor path can disappear from another angle, even when both figures fit on the plinth.

Can you start from a photograph?

Send a side view and a three-quarter view of the same subject when available. Identify one known length and which traits need to survive simplification. If you have a single photograph, circle the visible details you care about and identify the parts the sculptor can propose. Ask to review the proposed hidden side before the model is signed off.

Make the size line in the quote unambiguous

For a themed entrance, a useful request would read, “Match the body scale in the side reference. Keep the rearing pose. On the proposal, label the highest point, widest point and projection towards the path. Quote the base separately and show the artwork and base together in a front elevation.” Include the delivery city and whether visitors can approach from more than one side.

Frequently asked questions

Does life-size include the pedestal?

Specify the artwork height above its support and the total installed height from the finished ground. Listing the pedestal on its own line makes the two figures easy to compare.

Which horse dimension matters for a rearing sculpture?

Use a standing reference to agree body scale, then measure the proposed rearing pose to its highest and widest points. Include front-to-back projection and name the main viewing side.

How do I quote a pair of deer statues?

Give the supplier one labelled reference per deer, a plan showing their spacing and a front view with the tips of the antlers included. State whether the base is shared or separate.
