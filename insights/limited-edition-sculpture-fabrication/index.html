How to Fabricate a Limited-Edition Sculpture for Collectors

Published November 28, 2026By WEIERYANG
What should a collectible edition brief include?

An artist or gallery commissioning a collectible edition should identify the approved model, the authorised quantity, any separate proofs and the physical sample that controls the finish. Ask the fabricator to quote the one-time setup, the first piece and each later batch separately.

Give the workshop one master to work from

For a gallery shop or a retail art edition, record the model revision and its scale. If the artist approves a clay model and subsequently moves a hand on the digital file, label which version governs. Put the model photograph, dimensions and sign-off date on the same record.

Use a physical finish sample or an approved first piece. Mark the viewing face, signature position and an area where hand finishing may vary. The workshop portrait above illustrates exactly what a two-view record captures. A general description such as “hand painted” leaves the edge of an eye or a change in sheen open to interpretation.

Write down what each edition mark means

Agree the numbering convention in writing before anyone marks a piece. The example below uses an invented run of twelve so an artist, fabricator and gallery can test the register together.

Keep the register when a batch is split

Separate the authorised edition size from the number released for this order. A gallery might order three now and decide when to release the rest. Keep one row per piece with its proposed mark, production status, inspection photo, approval and packing reference. Assign the person who can release the next batch.

Agree how a rejected piece, replacement, additional proof and changes to a mould will be recorded. Ask the artist to confirm any repeat rights and who stores the master. The Musée Rodin describes the museum’s own controls for original bronze casts. Your edition agreement should state the controls for this particular work.

Compare the first-piece price and the repeat price

Separate model preparation, mould or tooling, first-piece approval and packaging from the unit cost of subsequent pieces. Ask what happens to the price if the gallery requests a second finish or a smaller second batch. Include the packing label and piece number in the proof you approve. This lets receiving staff match each object to the artist’s register when boxes arrive.

Frequently asked questions

Can the edition be made in separate batches?

Yes, when the artist and fabricator agree to it. Record the authorised total, pieces released so far, storage of the master and how later batches will be matched.

Who decides how many artist’s proofs there are?

The artist or rights holder and the commissioning parties should document that decision for the work. Write down proof quantities and markings before production.

What should I send for an edition quote?

Send model views, overall dimensions, intended material and finish, total edition size, first-batch quantity, marking instructions and the delivery location.
