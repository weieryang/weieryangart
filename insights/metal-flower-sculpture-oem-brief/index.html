Metal Flower Sculpture: An OEM Design & Fabrication Brief

Published November 5, 2026By WEIERYANGEnglish procurement guide
Direct answer

For an OEM metal flower sculpture, describe the bloom as a coordinated assembly: petal outline and depth, the important viewing faces, the proposed stem and base, the surface reference and the intended setting. Supply the current artwork with dimensions and mark unresolved design decisions. Agree who develops and reviews each interface before the design is released for fabrication.

This guide is for designers and procurement teams preparing a new flower-shaped commission. OEM here means fabrication to approved drawings and an agreed scope. A flower reference suggests a visual direction; it does not determine the material, support arrangement or suitability for a particular site.

Define the bloom before choosing a fabrication route

Start with the intended character: a recognisable botanical form, a simplified silhouette or an abstract group of petals. Identify which features must remain consistent and which the appointed designer may develop. Show the opening of the bloom, the depth between layers and how the centre meets the outer petals.

Label the front, rear and oblique views that matter at the proposed location. A flower beside a restaurant entrance may be seen from both sides; a feature against a wall may have a different review area. Name those views on the artwork so a convincing front render does not silently become approval of every surface.

Review petals, stems and bases as connected decisions

A petal change can affect more than the outline. Ask whether it changes the overall envelope, the adjoining centre or the connection to the stem. If the team proposes separate petals or a segmented stem, identify the parts and show where the joins will be visible. Review the appearance and assembly proposal together.

Ask the responsible designer to identify the technical inputs needed for the support review, including the proposed artwork mass, geometry and actual site conditions. Assign the design, checking, supply and installation responsibilities. A black display plinth in a photograph does not establish a foundation, an anchor design or what a new quotation includes.

Specify the surface on the faces that matter

Describe the desired colour, reflectivity, texture and treatment of visible joins. For a layered bloom, identify the upper and lower petal faces and the inner areas that also need review. Ask which reference will represent the proposed material and finish, rather than requesting an undefined “metallic look”.

If stainless steel is proposed, IMOA recommends defining the finish with the supplier, including relevant appearance characteristics and agreed visual references. Apply that discussion to the actual proposal; an attractive surface photograph does not confirm a grade or outdoor performance. The sculpture OEM sample guide helps distinguish a finish reference from a model or first piece.

Read production photographs for form and context

The petal-form production study shows a silver-coloured form, overlapping curves, openings and a black plinth. It is useful for discussing those visible relationships. Its photograph does not establish a botanical species, metal grade, dimensions or a completed venue installation.

The workshop image of curved components shows parts arranged on tables. Their final project use is not identified in this guide. These records can prompt a more precise request for views of your own proposed petal and stem interfaces; they are not represented as approved flower components or inspection acceptance.

Bring the location into the OEM brief

State whether the proposed work is inside, sheltered or exposed outdoors, and share the destination and site photographs. For outdoor work, identify known water, salt or cleaning exposure for the material review. Record surrounding circulation, reachable projecting parts and the access needed to maintain the piece. The project team must review these conditions for the actual design.

Choose the application route by the intended project use: a hotel garden belongs with hotel and commercial sculpture, while a shared public garden belongs with landscape and public art. For the wider delivery interfaces, use the overseas installation checklist.

Frequently asked questions

What should I send for a custom metal flower sculpture OEM review?

Send the current artwork and revision, intended overall dimensions and bloom depth, important viewing faces, the proposed stem and base relationship, destination and setting, and the material or finish questions still open. Identify who can review the design and who can provide the site information.

Can a flower photograph be used as the complete fabrication drawing?

It can explain a visual direction, but may leave side and rear geometry, petal depth, interfaces and dimensions unresolved. Identify the reference source and the decisions still needed, then agree who will develop and review the fabrication information.

Does the petal-form photograph prove that the sculpture is stainless steel?

No. The photograph shows a silver-coloured petal form, visible openings and a black plinth. It does not verify the composition or grade. Material information for a new metal flower sculpture must be specified and supported separately from its appearance reference.

Should the petals, stem and base be ordered as one piece?

Review that choice against the design, intended joins, transport route, positioning and site working area. If separate parts are proposed, identify each interface and agree the assembly and finish responsibilities. No standard section arrangement applies to every flower design.

Does an approved finish sample confirm outdoor suitability?

A finish review covers only its agreed scope. Record what material and surface the sample represents, and keep exposure conditions, material verification, structural interfaces and maintenance review as explicit project decisions. A sample photograph alone does not establish outdoor performance.
