Restaurant & Retail Sculpture OEM: Planning a Multi-Location Rollout

Published November 4, 2026By WEIERYANGEnglish procurement guide
Direct answer

For restaurant sculpture OEM projects across several locations, start with two connected records: a brand master that defines the shared appearance, and a location schedule that records what changes at each venue. Link every ordered piece to both records. Approving a character or abstract form for the brand does not approve its footprint, base, access route or installation at every store.

  • Define the brand master before listing store quantities
  • Give each location its own placement record
  • Test the schedule with three different placements
  • Connect repeat orders to the correct reference
  • Separate a brand change from a local change
  • Prepare a rollout brief for review

This guide is for restaurant operators, retail brands, designers and procurement teams planning custom sculptures for a proposed rollout. It provides a practical way to separate repeat design decisions from the site conditions that still need review.

Define the brand master before listing store quantities

The brand master is a controlled appearance reference. Give it an identifier and revision, then name the views and details it controls: silhouette, pose, expression, visible joins, colour references, surface appearance and the relationship to a plinth. Where a physical reference is required, identify the actual object and the decisions it is intended to support.

Also list permitted variations. A reversed pose, a different accessory or a smaller overall form can change the character's appearance. Treat those as separate proposals for review rather than allowing a factory or local store to interpret them independently. Artwork development and permission to commission that artwork should be resolved with the responsible brand team.

Agree how the appearance will be compared, which differences are acceptable and who can decide when a difference needs correction.

Give each location its own placement record

A restaurant entrance, service counter and retail window can use the same visual identity while requiring different pieces. The location record should explain the intended use and connect it to the current placement plan. Review the sculpture and plinth together, including projections, customer or staff routes, cleaning access and any proposed future relocation.

Test the schedule with three different placements

Illustrative planning example, not a WEIERYANG customer project: imagine a restaurant brand considering one character for a counter, an entrance and a shop window.

At the counter, the review centres on the customer-facing expression, occupied counter area and how staff can clean around the base. At the entrance, the team needs to review the complete footprint, approach views and the connection to the proposed support. In the window, the intended presentation may prioritise the street-facing view, while the maintenance team still needs access from inside.

These are three placement briefs, even if the same character artwork starts all of them. The schedule might identify a counter variant, an entrance variant and a window variant, each linked to its own drawing revision. If the entrance design changes, the counter and window versions should not silently inherit that change.

Connect repeat orders to the correct reference

For a later order, reference the model and revision being requested rather than writing “same as last time.” Compare the new site conditions with the earlier location record. Confirm whether the previous appearance reference remains available and whether the proposed materials, finish and assembly route are unchanged or need a new review.

A replacement also needs an identity. State whether it replaces the whole piece, a detachable component or a plinth. Record the unit it must match and the current condition of the retained parts. A photograph of an older installation can help explain appearance, but it does not establish concealed connections or the specification of a replacement.

Separate a brand change from a local change

A useful change note names the affected master or location revision, the units concerned and the next decision owner. A new brand colour direction may affect several proposed pieces. A revised doorway or display position may affect only one location. Distinguishing the two keeps an isolated site adjustment from becoming an unintended change to the whole series.

At agreed review points, request evidence that identifies the piece and revision being shown. The OEM fabrication evidence guide explains how to connect photographs to specific review questions; public workshop images alone are not inspection acceptance for a new order.

Prepare a rollout brief for review

Send the brand master, proposed variants, location schedule and known delivery sequence together. Mark undecided items clearly and identify who can resolve them. Explain whether this is a first location, a proposed repeat order or a replacement request so the scope can be reviewed accordingly.

Frequently asked questions

Can different store opening dates be included in one sculpture request?

Yes. List the proposed delivery sequence and the decision deadlines for each destination, and mark dates that remain provisional. Ask for a project-specific feasibility review; do not assume that one appearance approval establishes every fabrication or delivery date.

What if several designers or franchisees send conflicting comments?

Name the person responsible for consolidating brand comments and the person responsible for each site decision. Return one coordinated response tied to the relevant revision, with unresolved items visible, instead of treating separate messages as a single approval.

Should a rollout schedule include customer photographs?

Use photographs where they clarify placement or access, but share only material you are authorised to provide. Keep identifiable customer information and private receiving details out of public content; use the private project review channel for necessary project documents.

Does OEM mean that a standard order quantity or catalogue package applies?

No standard quantity, plinth package or installation service is assumed here. State the proposed quantities and destinations, then agree the design, fabrication and delivery scope for that request.
