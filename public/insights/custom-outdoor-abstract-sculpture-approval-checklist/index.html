Custom Outdoor Abstract Sculptures: OEM Approval Checklist

Published October 28, 2026By WEIERYANGBuyer worksheet
Direct answer

For custom outdoor abstract sculptures, record the exact drawing or sample revision, the question being reviewed, the named decision-maker and what may proceed next. OEM here means fabrication to approved client drawings. Appearance approval alone does not authorize engineering, fabrication or a later design change. Agree the review and release responsibilities for each project.

An email saying “looks good” can leave several decisions open. Use the proposed register below to turn a visual response into a specific, traceable instruction. It is a buyer worksheet to adapt with the project team, rather than a claim that every commission follows one WEIERYANG procedure.

For fabrication scope, see custom sculpture from client drawings. Outdoor exposure, material selection and site interfaces belong in your project-specific review.

1. Identify the version being reviewed

Name the artwork, intended location and current drawing or model revision. Include the controlling units and views needed to understand the form. Identify the client-authorized design basis; a photograph that may be displayed is not automatically permission to fabricate the pictured work. Keep superseded files available for reference, clearly marked so they cannot be mistaken for current instructions.

2. Separate review from permission to proceed

Name the people responsible for appearance, project-specific technical interfaces and authorization of the next activity. Their roles may be held by different parties. Ask each reviewer a defined question: reviewing the silhouette does not settle a base connection. Record the permitted next step and anything still on hold, even when the design response is positive. Local engineering and site responsibilities require their own appointed reviewers.

3. Define the form and finish comparison

In the gold-coloured abstract production photograph, look at the changing curves, openings and reflected lines. In the warm-coloured work, compare the curved form with the rectangular plinth. For your commission, identify which views and finish reference will govern the appearance review. A finish sample cannot settle the entire geometry, and a model image cannot verify a physical surface. These photographs establish neither material grade nor outdoor suitability.

4. Make every register row answer one question

Connect the file and revision to a specific review question, evidence reference, reviewer name and role, status and decision date. Then state the permitted next step, held scope and written decision reference. Keep a change-impact field and a re-review owner. “Reviewed” is incomplete unless the reader can see what was reviewed and what the decision allows. Agree status labels with the team; silence is not a release.

6. Reopen affected decisions when the design changes

Log the proposed change against the previous revision. Identify affected pieces, views, finish references, base or site interfaces, and any scope or programme questions. Ask the relevant named reviewers which decisions must be repeated. Issue the revised package with its change record and obtain authorization for the affected next activity. Approval of an earlier version does not automatically carry over; record even a decision that no further review is needed.

7. Start with a genuinely blank review register

Download the blank CSV register and use one row per review decision. It has thirteen field labels and one empty row, with no example approvals, customer data or preselected status. Fill references from your actual package. Leave unknown values blank and state the unresolved question; do not mark them accepted. Link the written decision and confirm the authorized next activity before treating the row as closed.

8. Keep the approval record with the delivery package

Before packing, compare the current register with the latest files and agreed evidence. Reconcile open changes and unresolved scope with the responsible parties. The register identifies decisions; it does not replace a packing plan, site engineering or the contract. Use the existing RFQ scope template for commercial inclusions and exclusions. Share the current drawings and open review questions through the original project brief.

OEM drawing and sample approval questions

Can one drawing approval release the whole sculpture for production?

Only if the project’s authorized parties explicitly release that scope. A visual review may cover form while technical interfaces or other documents remain open. Record the permitted activity and held scope separately.

Does approving a finish sample approve the shape and plinth?

It may approve only the stated sample characteristics. Identify whether form, views, plinth relationship and interfaces were reviewed separately; do not extend a surface decision to unreviewed parts.

What if our designer changes the sculpture after approval?

Record the new revision and affected decisions. Ask the relevant reviewers what must be checked again, then obtain the authorization required for the affected work. An earlier approval does not automatically cover a change.

Can photographs replace the approval package?

Photographs can support a defined review when tied to the current reference. They do not, by themselves, prove dimensions, material grade, outdoor suitability, concealed interfaces or authority to release fabrication.

Is the downloadable register a quotation or signed acceptance?

No. It is a blank decision-record worksheet. Complete it with your actual project references and authorized roles, and link the written decisions. Use the separate RFQ template to compare quotation scope.

Public commissioning background

Public programs illustrate the value of defined review stages. GSA’s 2022 Art in Architecture policies distinguish preliminary and final concept review. New York City’s artwork guidance separates review stages and addresses amended submissions after changes. These are examples for their respective programs, not WEIERYANG procedures or evidence that a private project meets their requirements.
