Layered Horse Sculpture: Fabrication Review

Read the workshop photograph through its horizontal profiles, reflective surfaces and base, then prepare the drawings and approvals for your own layered sculpture.

Article published: 2026-10-22

Read the silhouette and the layers

The workshop photograph shows a horse form built visually from horizontal profiles, with reflective surfaces and a supporting base. The spaces between the layers change how the silhouette reads. This record does not establish a material grade or physical dimensions.

Agree the views before fabrication

For a new OEM commission, provide approved front, side and angled views, together with intended viewing distances. Ask the project team to review profile outlines, visible gaps and surface direction together, rather than approving one attractive photograph as a complete specification.

Define the base and handling scope

Include the base arrangement, proposed connections and access constraints in the new brief. Agree which drawings require approval and how transport, handling and site assembly will be addressed. The workshop setting does not show a completed installation or establish those responsibilities.

Prepare a brief for your own project

  • Approved artwork and views, overall dimensions and intended viewing distances.
  • Required finish sample and the profile or layer details to be reviewed.
  • Base interface, destination, delivery access and agreed local installation responsibilities.

The article records what the supplied photograph shows and what to review for a new commission. Material specifications, dimensions, artwork rights and delivery scope must be agreed for your project.

Questions before fabrication

Is this the same work as the outdoor reflective horse?

No. The layered workshop horse and the reflective rearing horse shown outdoors are different works. Their photographs are not presented as stages of one project.

Can the photograph define an exact OEM reproduction?

The photograph can support discussion. A new commission needs artwork you are authorised to use, approved drawings, dimensions, materials, finish and a written fabrication scope.
